Built · Nov 6

Gas-free checkout, end to end

The payer never holds WBT, writes a seed phrase or picks a chain. They sign one off-chain approval; Zeeto's relayer submits it through the ZeetoCheckout contract and pays the gas for the business. Proven against the live Whitechain USDT on a fork: a real payment and refund settled with exactly these signatures.

1Order + intent

Server prices it and returns a USDT ReceiveWithAuthorization: from the customer, to ZeetoCheckout, exact amount, 10-minute window, nonce = payNonce(order, stall).

2Customer signs

Embedded wallet, wallet pop-ups off. The screen says Pay ₹120. Nothing costs gas.

3Checks before gas

Signer, amount, nonce, window, unused, balance, limits, then a simulation. Fails cost nothing.

4Relayer → contract

ZeetoCheckout.pay pulls the USDT and forwards all of it to the stall, emits Paid. The server only marks paid after reading that event.

Who pays gas, and why it can't be drained

The business covers it, with hard limits

  • Business-paid. Every relayed payment and refund is logged with its gas and stall: shown on the merchant's Developers page and in the accountant CSV. Testnet: topped up from the faucet. Production: billed per payment.
  • Only its own intents. The relayer submits only approvals the server issued, for orders it created, signed by that order's customer (or the stall, for refunds).
  • Limits. Min ₹10 · 25 per customer/day · 500 per stall/day · 0.5 WBT/day budget · gas-price ceiling. Holds WBT only, never USDT.
  • Worst case = one day's budget. Then customers fall back to submitting the same signature themselves. Admin shows balance, low-balance alert and every relayed tx.
ZeetoCheckout · Whitechain Sepolia

Small contract, strong rules

  • Stall bound in the signature (nonce = order + stall): a relayer can't redirect money or reuse a signature.
  • One payment per (order, payer): front-running with your own money can't block someone's order.
  • Refunds signed by the stall, only to the original payer, capped at what was paid, unique nonce each.
  • receiveWithAuthorization: only the payee contract can use the signature, so the mempool can't front-run it into a plain transfer.
  • No custody, no withdraw. Owner can pause new payments only; refunds always work. 15 unit tests + live-USDT fork test.
Drop-in for any store

Checkout API + checkout.js, about 20 lines

  • POST /api/checkout with the stall's secret key → session URL. GET it back for status, payer, tx and explorer link.
  • checkout.js: Zeeto.button('#pay', { createSession, onPaid }) opens the hosted page in a popup.
  • Signed webhooks: payment.succeeded / payment.refunded with Zeeto-Signature (HMAC). Keys shown once, stored hashed.
  • examples/store: a chai shop's own website taking Zeeto payments.

The everyday case · built

Walk up, ask, scan, pay. No typing.

Most people won’t pre-order. They go to the stall, ask for three things, and pay. Zeeto works exactly like a UPI QR at the counter, except the payment is USDT on Whitechain and both phones confirm it.

1Ask in person

“Two kilo tomatoes, one palak, one dhaniya.” Nothing to type.

2Stall rings it up

Types ₹76 on the keypad, or taps the items. Taps Show QR.

3Customer scans with the camera

Sees the stall’s name and photo, the locked amount, taps Pay 0.90 USDT.

4Both phones confirm

Stall’s phone says “Zeeto par 76 rupaye mile”. Customer sees a green tick with a live clock.

Two QR codes

Exact bill, or a printed sticker

  • Bill QR on the stall’s phone (recommended). Amount and items locked by the stall. Works once, expires in 5 minutes. /sell/collect
  • Printed shop QR on the stall. Opens “Pay Ravi’s Fresh Sabzi”, the customer types the amount they were told, or taps “order ahead” instead. /p/ravi
Counter fraud, handled

Swapped stickers and fake screenshots

  • Swapped QR sticker. The QR only names the stall. Name, photo and wallet come from Zeeto, so a fake sticker shows a different shop, and money can only go to the registered wallet.
  • Old screenshot of “paid”. The receipt has a live ticking clock, and the stall trusts its own phone’s sound, not the customer’s screen.
  • Paying twice. Each bill QR settles once. Opening it again just shows “paid”.
  • Wrong amount. Capped at ₹20,000 per payment; the stall can refund from Payments.

WhatsApp · built, needs Meta keys

The same Zeeto, inside the app everyone already has

Two conversations on one Zeeto business number. A repair shop runs its bookings by chatting. A customer orders from a chemist by chatting, with a pharmacist checking the prescription, and pays in USDT through a secure Zeeto link. The wallet never lives inside WhatsApp.

Can customers pay with crypto inside WhatsApp?

Order in WhatsApp, pay through a Zeeto link

  • WhatsApp can’t hold a wallet. The Business API sends messages and buttons. It can’t sign transactions or keep keys.
  • WhatsApp Pay in India is UPI only. Its payment features run through Meta’s approved gateways in rupees, not crypto.
  • Meta’s commerce policy bars selling crypto. Zeeto sells vegetables and chai. USDT is only how the bill is settled, on our own page. Check this with Meta before launch.
  • So the button opens Zeeto. Same phone number → same Privy login → same wallet. Pay, and the receipt comes back into the chat.
Safety on the pay link

A WhatsApp link can’t be used against you

  • Single order, short life. The link points to one order and expires in 15 minutes, same as the signed payment intent.
  • Locked to the number. Login needs a code sent to the WhatsApp number that placed the order.
  • Price can’t change. The amount and stall come from the signed intent, not from the link.
  • Receipts come from the chain. “Paid” in the chat is sent only after the API reads the Paid event.

Safety rules

What the system will never do

Mark an order paid because the browser said so. Only the API, after reading the chain.
Hold anyone’s money or keys. The contract forwards 100% to the stall in the same transaction; Privy holds signing keys; the relayer holds only WBT.
Let AI act alone. It drafts; the vendor confirms; the API validates prices and quantities.
Move money from WhatsApp. Refunds and withdrawals need the app and the stall’s wallet.
Let the client set a price, wallet or role. Amounts come from the database, wallets from Privy, roles from the server.
Show a mock as real, or crypto words to a payer. Demo values are labelled; real stalls show “Pay ₹120” and “Paid”.
